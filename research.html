Dermatology Research

California Dermatology is actively involved in clinical research to help advance the understanding and treatment of skin disease. At the forefront of dermatology innovation, our research team is dedicated to discovering groundbreaking treatments that change lives. Patients can join this adventure through clinical trials and long-term observational studies—gaining access to cutting-edge medications, top-tier medical care, and stipends per visit, all at no cost. With a vision that reaches beyond conventional care, we're pushing boundaries to bring hope and healing to patients with these skin conditions everywhere. If you or your child has one of the conditions below, you may be eligible to participate in one of our research studies.

Acne

Acne can range from persistent blackheads and pimples to deeper, painful breakouts that may lead to scarring. We participate in clinical research evaluating new treatment options for patients with acne.


Alopecia Areata

Alopecia areata is an autoimmune condition that causes patchy or more extensive hair loss on the scalp or body. Our research includes studies involving adolescents with alopecia areata to better understand treatment outcomes and the long-term effects of the condition.


Atopic Dermatitis (Eczema)

Atopic dermatitis is a chronic inflammatory skin condition that causes dry, itchy, irritated skin and can significantly affect sleep and quality of life. We have multiple studies for adolescents and adults with moderate-to-severe eczema, including research evaluating new treatments as well as treatments used in routine dermatologic care.


Bullous Pemphigoid

A rare blistering disorder, typically in older adults, where the immune system attacks the skin, forming large, tense blisters. Our trials investigate innovative treatments to reduce blistering and improve skin resilience.


Chronic Hand Eczema

Chronic hand eczema can cause persistent redness, itching, scaling, cracking, and discomfort of the hands. Our research examines how chronic hand eczema affects patients over time and how well different treatments work in everyday clinical practice.


Chronic Hives

Chronic spontaneous urticaria (CSU) causes recurring itchy, raised welts or hives without an obvious trigger. Our research includes adults whose hives remain uncontrolled despite antihistamine treatment and evaluates treatment response, long-term outcomes, and quality of life.


Chronic Itching (Pruritus)

Chronic pruritus is persistent itching that may occur without a clear underlying cause and can have a major impact on sleep and daily life. We participate in clinical research evaluating potential new treatments for patients with ongoing, unexplained itching.


Cutaneous Lupus

A form of lupus affecting the skin, causing red, scaly rashes or sores, often worsened by sun exposure. Our research seeks novel approaches to manage symptoms and protect against flare-ups.


Cutaneous Sarcoidosis

Cutaneous sarcoidosis occurs when sarcoidosis affects the skin, causing bumps, plaques, discoloration, or other persistent skin changes. We participate in clinical research evaluating potential new treatments for patients with skin involvement from sarcoidosis.


Dermatomyositis

Dermatomyositis is a rare inflammatory disease characterized by distinctive rashes across the eyelids, knuckles, or chest, frequently accompanied by progressive muscle weakness. Our research evaluates novel therapies and clinical strategies aimed at controlling inflammation, easing cutaneous symptoms, and improving muscle strength and quality of life.


Gorlin Syndrome

Gorlin syndrome, also called basal cell nevus syndrome, is a rare genetic condition that can cause patients to develop multiple basal cell carcinomas over their lifetime. Our research includes studies evaluating new approaches to treating basal cell carcinomas in patients with Gorlin syndrome.


Hidradenitis Suppurativa

A painful, chronic condition featuring inflamed, boil-like lumps in areas like the armpits or groin due to blocked hair follicles. Our studies focus on cutting-edge options to ease discomfort and prevent scarring.


Prurigo Nodularis

Prurigo nodularis is a chronic skin condition that causes intensely itchy, firm bumps or nodules and can lead to repeated scratching and skin damage. Our research focuses on treatments that may reduce itching, improve the skin, and improve patients’ quality of life.


Psoriasis

Psoriasis is a chronic inflammatory condition that causes red, thick, or scaly areas of skin and can affect many parts of the body. California Dermatology participates in multiple psoriasis studies, including research for plaque psoriasis and psoriasis involving the palms of the hands and soles of the feet, with studies of both established and investigational treatments.


Vitiligo

Vitiligo causes areas of the skin to lose pigment, resulting in lighter or white patches. Our research includes adolescents and adults with nonsegmental vitiligo and examines treatment outcomes, changes in the condition over time, and its effect on quality of life.

Interested in Participating?

Clinical research studies have specific eligibility requirements that vary from study to study. If you or your child has one of the conditions listed above, contact our research team to learn whether one of our studies may be a good fit.


Our team can answer your questions, review your medical and treatment history, and provide more information about available research opportunities.
